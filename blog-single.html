Dry Hopping Techniques: Cold Side vs. Warm Side — 12 Batches, One Winner

Dave Keller
Co-Founder · Head Brewmaster
August 28, 2026

Every homebrewer who has chased the juicy, tropical aroma of a great NEIPA has encountered the same question: when exactly do you add the dry hops, and at what temperature? The answer, it turns out, is more nuanced than the forums would have you believe.

Over the past six months, our brew team ran a structured experiment: 12 parallel batches of the same West Coast IPA recipe, split between two dry hopping protocols. Here's everything we found.

The Setup

All 12 batches used identical grain bills, hop additions, and yeast pitch rates. The only variable was the dry hopping protocol:

  • Cold-side dry hopping: Hops added at 35°F / 2°C, after fermentation was fully complete, for 5 days.
  • Warm-side dry hopping: Hops added at 68°F / 20°C, while the beer was still at fermentation temperature, during active yeast activity.

We evaluated each batch across four criteria: perceived aroma intensity, flavor integration, clarity, and shelf-stability at 4 weeks post-packaging.

Warm-Side Results

Warm-side dry hopping produced dramatically more intense tropical and citrus aroma in the first 1–2 weeks post-packaging. The active yeast interacts with the hops through a process called biotransformation, converting certain hop compounds into more volatile aroma-active forms.

"The warm-side batches smelled like you punched a mango. Incredible aroma intensity right out of the fermenter."

However, by week four, those same batches showed noticeable aroma fade and an oxidative character that wasn't present at week one. The high activity environment accelerates hop compound volatilization — a double-edged sword.

Cold-Side Results

Cold-side hops produced more restrained but remarkably stable aroma profiles. The tropical notes were present but more integrated — more like "the beer has hop flavor" rather than "the beer smells like raw hops." At week four, cold-side batches still had fresh, vibrant aroma with minimal drop-off.

The Verdict

It depends on your goal:

  • Brewing for a competition or event within 2 weeks of packaging? Use warm-side dry hopping for maximum aroma impact.
  • Brewing to drink at leisure over a month or two? Cold-side dry hopping delivers better staying power.
  • Best of both worlds? A two-stage protocol — warm-side first (at the tail end of fermentation) followed by a cold-side addition — combines biotransformation with stability. This is our new house protocol.

Practical Recommendations

  • Use hop rates of 1–2 oz per gallon for either approach. More hops do not linearly increase aroma.
  • Purge your fermenter with CO₂ before opening. Oxygen is the enemy of dry hop aroma.
  • For the two-stage protocol: add 60% at 68°F on Day 3 of fermentation, add remaining 40% cold after crashing.
  • Citra, Mosaic, and El Dorado perform exceptionally well with warm-side biotransformation.

Happy brewing. Go make something great.
